How to choose a Denver SEO company: 12 checks you can run on their own site in 10 minutes.

Lists and sales calls all sound the same. An agency’s own website and public footprint do not lie. Here is what to check, with the free tools to do it.

To choose a Denver SEO company, check the agency’s own website before you take a sales call: speed, structured data, indexed pages, recent reviews, published pricing and contract terms tell you more than the pitch will. The twelve checks below take about ten minutes, most run free in a browser, and they work on every Denver SEO company, agency or consultant, including us.

Search “Denver SEO companies” and you get list sites, review marketplaces and thirty agencies that all promise page one. The lists are paid or pay-to-play more often than not, and every agency page reads the same. What does not lie is the agency’s own site and its public footprint. An SEO company that cannot get its own house in order will not get yours in order either.

Twelve checks, in the order we would run them

1. Their own site speed

Run their homepage through PageSpeed Insights on mobile. Core Web Vitals are a confirmed ranking signal and a conversion factor. A Denver SEO company scoring in the 40s on its own homepage is telling you what its engineering is worth.

2. Their structured data

Paste their URL into our free schema validator. You want an Organization or LocalBusiness with a real address and phone, and no errors. Missing or broken schema on an SEO agency’s own site is the equivalent of a mechanic driving on bald tires.

3. What they let AI crawlers read

Use the AI crawler checker on their domain. If an agency sells “AI search optimization” while its robots.txt blocks the crawlers that feed ChatGPT and Perplexity, the service is a slide deck.

4. How many of their pages Google actually indexes

Search site:theirdomain.com. Hundreds of near-identical city pages for places they have never worked is the doorway pattern Google’s spam policy names. A smaller, specific site is the better sign.

5. Reviews: recency, not just count

Open their Google Business Profile. Look at the dates. Sixty reviews from 2019 and none since says the client base turned over. Also read the three-star reviews; they are the honest ones.

6. Case studies with a baseline

“Traffic up 400%” is meaningless without the starting number, the time span and whether calls or revenue moved. Ask for one case in your industry with before and after figures and a client you can phone.

7. Published pricing or published ranges

Agencies that publish ranges are confident in their value. Agencies that hide every number until a discovery call are pricing to the prospect. Compare any quote against the Denver SEO pricing ranges on this site.

8. Contract terms

Month to month with written notice is the standard confident agencies offer. Twelve-month minimums with auto-renewal protect the agency, not you. Ask who owns the content, the accounts and the data if you leave; the answer should be “you” every time.

9. Who does the work

Ask the person on the sales call whether they will be on your account and who writes the content. A named, reachable strategist beats a rotating pod. Offshore content farms are not a crime, but you should know you are paying for one.

10. What they say about links

If the proposal includes a monthly backlink quota, walk. Paid and templated links are the fastest way to get an established site devalued. Earned mentions from local news, suppliers and associations are slow, and they are the only kind worth paying for.

11. Their first-month plan for your site

Before signing, ask what they would fix first and why. A real answer names specific pages, technical faults and competitors from your market. A generic answer means nobody looked at your site.

12. Measurement

Ask what you will see each month and where. The right answer includes Search Console, call tracking or at least phone-click events, and a short list of what changed and why. A 40-page PDF of rankings for terms you never chose is a smoke machine.

Green flags, in one paragraph

A fast, specific website. Recent reviews. One case study with real numbers and a referee. Published ranges. Month-to-month terms and you own everything. A named person who has looked at your site before the call. Measurement you can open yourself. That is the whole test, and most Denver SEO companies pass about half of it.

FAQ

Questions, answered straight

How do I pick between Denver SEO companies?

Check each agency's own website and public footprint before the sales call: mobile speed, valid structured data, what AI crawlers can read, how many pages are indexed, review recency, case studies with baselines, published pricing, contract terms, who does the work, their stance on links, a first-month plan specific to your site, and what you will see in reporting.

What is a red flag in an SEO proposal?

A monthly backlink quota, a ranking guarantee, a twelve-month minimum with auto-renewal, pricing with no hours attached, a plan that could apply to any website, and reporting that is a rankings PDF rather than calls, forms and Search Console data.

Are 'best Denver SEO companies' lists trustworthy?

Treat them as a starting list, not a ranking. Many are paid placements or ranked by review volume on the list site itself. Run the twelve checks on any agency you find there.

Should I hire a Denver agency or a national one?

Either can work. What matters is whether someone who understands the Denver market will actually be on your account, and whether they can show results for a business like yours. Local knowledge of neighborhoods, competitors and the map pack helps; a national firm that assigns a junior pod does not.

How much should a Denver SEO company cost?

Most campaigns run $500 to $5,000 a month depending on competition and geography. Our Denver SEO pricing page lists the ranges by situation and what each buys.
