Website redesign SEO means protecting and improving search visibility while a site's design, content or structure changes. It covers benchmarking current rankings, keeping or redirecting every indexed URL, preserving the content and internal links that earn traffic, and testing the new site for crawlability, speed and structured data before it launches.

Redesigns are exciting. New colors, new photos, a fresh homepage. Then the new navigation quietly drops the page that brought in a third of your leads, a copywriter trims the paragraph that ranked for your best keyword, and the developer changes every URL "to make it cleaner." Nobody did anything wrong on purpose. Nobody was assigned to protect the traffic.

That is our job on a redesign. Your designer makes it beautiful. We make sure Google, AI assistants and your customers can still find it, and that it loads fast enough to keep them.

What a redesign should improve, not just preserve

  • Speed: a redesign is the cheapest moment to fix Core Web Vitals. See our page speed optimization work.
  • Structure: cleaner topic clusters and internal links that pass authority to your money pages.
  • AI readiness: direct answers, FAQ and schema that assistants can quote. Our answer engine optimization page explains how.
  • Conversion: the calls to action, forms and phone number placement that turn visits into revenue.

FAQ

Questions, answered straight

Does a website redesign affect SEO?

Yes. Changes to URLs, content, internal links, speed and structured data all affect rankings. A planned redesign can improve SEO; an unplanned one often causes a traffic drop that takes months to recover.

How do I redesign a website without losing SEO?

Benchmark current performance, keep or redirect every indexed URL, preserve the content that ranks, test the new site on staging, submit a new sitemap at launch and monitor Search Console closely afterward.

Should I change my URLs during a redesign?

Only if there is a real benefit. Every URL change needs a permanent redirect, and fewer changes mean less risk.

Can you work with my designer or agency?

Yes. We often handle the SEO and technical side while another team handles design. We join early, map the risks and QA the build before launch.
