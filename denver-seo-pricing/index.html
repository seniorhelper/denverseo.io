Where the hours go

SEO is priced by people-time, and the useful work is slow. A $1,200 retainer at a competent Denver shop is roughly ten to fifteen hours a month. Here is what those hours look like when the work is real:

  • Technical health (2–4 hours). Crawl errors, indexing, Core Web Vitals, schema that still validates after a plugin update, forms that still send. Boring, and the first thing that breaks.
  • Google Business Profile (1–3 hours). Posts, Q&A, photos, categories, replying to reviews, fighting suggested edits. This is most of the map-pack result for a local business.
  • Content (3–6 hours). One genuinely useful page or a real improvement to an existing one. Not four 400-word posts nobody will read.
  • Authority (1–3 hours). Earning mentions from local news, suppliers, associations and chambers. Slow by design. Anyone selling “50 backlinks a month” is selling you a problem.
  • Measurement and conversion (1–2 hours). Call tracking, form tests, Search Console review, what to change next.

When a quote comes in far below the market range, one of those lines is missing. Usually it is content and authority, which are the two that actually move a competitive term.

Five ways to check any Denver SEO quote

  1. Ask for the hours. Not the deliverables list, the hours. Divide the price by the hours. Under $75 an hour for senior work in Denver is not senior work.
  2. Ask who touches your account. A named person who has done this before, or a queue? The price should reflect the answer.
  3. Ask what happens in month one. A good answer names specific problems on your site. A generic answer means they have not looked.
  4. Read the exit terms. Twelve-month contracts with auto-renewal are a pricing device. Month to month with written notice is what confident agencies offer.
  5. Check their own site. If a Denver SEO company’s own pages are slow, thin or missing schema, that is the quality you are buying. Our free tools run those checks in a browser.

What a budget has to produce to pay for itself

Skip the projected-return charts. Do the arithmetic backwards: monthly budget divided by what one customer is worth gives customers needed per month; divide that by your close rate for leads needed. A $1,500 retainer, $1,000 customers and a 25% close rate means six leads a month to break even. If six extra leads a month is implausible in your market, the budget is wrong no matter who is quoting it. If it is easy, SEO is cheap for you and the question is only who does it well.

How much does SEO cost in Denver per month?

Most Denver campaigns run $500 to $5,000 a month. A single-location service business in a mid-competition category usually pays $800 to $1,500; contested metro categories run $2,000 to $3,000; regional or multi-location work is $3,000 to $5,000 and up. Technical projects are fixed fees.

Can I start SEO for under $500 a month?

Yes, if the scope is honest. Eye To Ad Media sells build-your-own levels from $100 a month in $100 steps, each covering one area done properly, such as your Google Business Profile or one page cluster. What does an SEO audit cost?

It depends on depth. An automated report is cheap or free, a checklist review takes hours, and a full technical audit of a larger or JavaScript-heavy site takes days. We quote audits as fixed prices after a free review call. See our SEO audit cost page.

How long before SEO pays for itself?

Local terms typically move in three to six months and competitive terms in six to twelve. Work out the break-even first: budget divided by customer value gives customers needed, divided by close rate gives leads needed. If that lead count is realistic for your market, the timeline is the only remaining risk.

Because the real answer depends on your domain's age and authority, how contested your category is, the condition of your site and how much geography you cover. That is legitimate.
